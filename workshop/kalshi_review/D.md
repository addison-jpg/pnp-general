# Panopticon, signs, and simulacra: reading notes

Every item below is labelled: reading, analogy, no check. Sources were found by standard web search. Quoted claims and page numbers were not checked against the books. "Reference not confirmed" marks anything I could not tie to a page or chapter.

## 1. Panopticon

- **Bentham (1791), reading, analogy, no check.** Bentham's plan is a circular prison with cells around the perimeter and a central inspection tower, so that inmates may be watched while the inspector is not visible to them. The point is that inmates cannot tell when they are watched. Publication confirmed: *Panopticon: or, the Inspection-House* (Letters and Postscripts, 1791). Stable source: UCL Bentham Project, https://www.ucl.ac.uk/bentham-project/who-was-jeremy-bentham/panopticon. Page reference: reference not confirmed.

- **Foucault, *Discipline and Punish* (French 1975; Eng. Sheridan, Vintage 1995), reading, analogy, no check.** Foucault reads the plan as a general model of disciplinary power: visibility becomes a means of control, and the subject internalises being watched, so the power works without a watcher present. Chapter confirmed: Part Three, ch. 3, "Panopticism," pp. 195–228. Page numbers for specific claims: reference not confirmed. Publisher or library page: reference not confirmed. A course-hosted copy exists at https://crysp.uwaterloo.ca/courses/survpriv/F20/cache/foucault.info/documents/foucault.disciplineAndPunish.panOpticism/index.html (not a publisher page).

## 2. Signs and symbols

- **Saussure, *Course in General Linguistics* (1916, posthumous), reading, analogy, no check.** The sign is a dyad of signifier (form) and signified (concept), and their link is arbitrary, held in place by social convention. Chapter reference: reference not confirmed. Stable URL: reference not confirmed.

- **Peirce, sign theory, reading, analogy, no check.** The sign is triadic: representamen, object, and interpretant, with the sign standing for its object to an interpreter in some respect. Quotation at CP 2.228: reference not confirmed. Secondary overview only: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_(semiotics) (not for citation). Mapping of Peirce onto Saussure is contested by sources.

- **Baudrillard, *Simulacra and Simulation* (French 1981; Eng. Glaser, Univ. of Michigan Press 1994), reading, analogy, no check.** Chapter "The Precession of Simulacra" argues that signs and models come to precede and replace the real they once represented, illustrated by the map that comes before the territory. Chapter confirmed; page numbers: reference not confirmed. Publisher page: reference not confirmed.

## Possible analogy to prediction markets

Reading: a published price that every participant can see works like the central tower, since traders may adjust to being observed without knowing who is actually reading their orders.
Reading: Foucault's point that visibility produces self-discipline maps onto participants who trade in line with a displayed price or crowd view rather than their own information.
Reading: Baudrillard's precession of simulacra suggests the opposite worry, that a price can come to stand in for the event it was meant to measure, so the signal replaces the outcome.
