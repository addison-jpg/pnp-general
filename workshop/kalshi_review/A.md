# Academic studies: prediction markets and public opinion / voter behaviour

Scope: search-only review (WebSearch, standard mode; no full texts opened) covering Kalshi, PredictIt, Polymarket and Iowa Electronic Markets (IEM). Most hits measure accuracy, not opinion or behaviour effects.

## Sources

1. **Berg, Nelson & Rietz (2008).** "Prediction market accuracy in the long run." *International Journal of Forecasting* 24(2): 285–300. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intfor:v:24:y:2008:i:2:p:285-300
   - Label: published, peer-reviewed
   - Finding: IEM "significantly outperforms the polls in every election" more than 100 days out; closer to outcome 74% of the time.
   - Design: IEM vote-share markets vs national polls, US presidential elections 1988–2004. Accuracy only.

2. **Gruca & Rietz (2025).** "Iowa Electronic Markets: Forecasting the 2024 U.S. Presidential Election." *PS: Political Science & Politics* 58(2): 258–266. https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/B256F4EA39F7F9B6F0423BA4EDA46B4A
   - Label: published, peer-reviewed
   - Finding: presents IEM forecasts for 2024 popular-vote shares. Abstract not opened; based on title and search summary.
   - Design: IEM market, 2024 US election.

3. **Cutting et al. (2025).** "Are Betting Markets Better than Polling in Predicting Political Elections?" arXiv:2507.08921. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08921
   - Label: published, preprint
   - Finding: Polymarket outperformed polls, especially in swing states.
   - Design: Polymarket prices vs polls, 2024 US election. Accuracy only.

4. **Tsang & Yang (2026).** "Anatomy of Polymarket." arXiv:2603.03136. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03136
   - Label: published, preprint
   - Finding: tracks volume, arbitrage gaps and price impact, and discusses trader disagreement.
   - Design: on-chain transaction data, 2024 election market. No voter data.

5. **Brennan Center for Justice (2026).** "New study finds prediction markets are influencing voters and voters want..." https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/new-study-finds-prediction-markets-are-influencing-voters-and-voters-want
   - Label: report, not peer-reviewed
   - Finding (per search summary of the page): Kalshi odds raised perceived electability; 84% odds added about 20 points; over 80% wanted tighter regulation or a ban.
   - Design: survey experiment, 1,994 registered voters. Published by an advocacy organisation.

6. **Authors not retrieved (2026).** "Manipulation in Prediction Markets: An Agent-based Modeling Experiment." arXiv:2601.20452. https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20452
   - Label: published, preprint
   - Finding: whale traders can temporarily shift prices and add volatility before the market adjusts. Summary says it links this to voter beliefs; not checked in full text.
   - Design: agent-based simulation of a binary election market. Not empirical.

## Not found

- No peer-reviewed study of Kalshi or PredictIt effects on public opinion, belief or voter behaviour.
- No field or natural experiment measuring voters' exposure to market prices.
- Clinton & Huang (Vanderbilt) accuracy study of Polymarket, Kalshi and PredictIt: known only from a DL News report; paper not located. Omitted.
- Mantovani & Filippin, *Games and Economic Behavior* (lab study of belief updating from prices): no URL retrieved. Omitted.
- Forsythe, Rietz & Ross (1999), *JEBO*; Berg & Rietz (2014), *PS*: no URL retrieved. Omitted.
