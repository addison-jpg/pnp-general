# Prediction markets and public opinion, read through Marx, the panopticon and signs

Status: question and hypotheses only. Nothing is checked; no asteroid run was made. Links to Marxism, the panopticon, signs or the social model are labelled **analogy**.

## Question

Does a published prediction-market price (for example, a Kalshi contract) relate to public opinion, and for whom? Can that relation be read through ideology, commodity fetishism, alienation, the panopticon, signs and the social model? The last five are formal analogy only.

## Empirical hypotheses (open)

**H1 (influence).** Showing a market price changes stated opinion or perceived electability.
- Refuted by: a randomized exposure experiment with a no-price control, showing no difference beyond a preset margin.
- Data needed: randomized design; full questionnaire; sample frame; funder and vendor disclosure.

**H2 (tracking).** Market prices lead or track poll aggregates on the same item.
- Refuted by: no lead-lag at any tested horizon, or failure out of sample.
- Data needed: time-stamped prices; poll dates, toplines and wording; resolved outcomes.

## Disabled participants (open)

Disabled people work every day, so this section is not optional.

**D1 (access).** Barriers to market use (interfaces, identity checks, funding rules) fall harder on disabled traders, so price formation draws on a narrower group.
- Refuted by: participation and trade volume matching population disability rates, after controlling for income.
- Data needed: platform accessibility audits; self-reported disability; income by disability status.
- Published context (BLS, *People with a Disability: Labor Force Characteristics, 2025*, released 3 March 2026; search summary only, page not opened): employment-population ratio 22.8% with a disability, 65.2% without; 38.1% at ages 16 to 64 with a disability; unemployment 8.3% against 4.1%; about 30% against 17% usually part time. October 2025 was not collected, so 2025 is not strictly comparable with other years. These figures count paid work only, not unpaid work, and say nothing about market participation.

**D2 (sampling).** Polls under-sample disabled respondents, so H2's opinion side misses them.
- Refuted by: poll samples matching census disability figures, or weighting that leaves toplines unchanged.
- Data needed: weighting schemes; disability status where collected; census benchmarks.

**D3 (social model, analogy).** Barriers, not only impairment, make a price hard to read, so its meaning depends on who can read it.
- Refuted by: identical comprehension and trading for disabled and nondisabled users of the same interface.
- Data needed: D1 audits; comprehension tests with screen-reader and plain-language users.

## Analogy hypotheses (labelled analogy, no check)

**A1 (ideology, commodity fetishism).** A displayed price presents aggregated opinion as a property of the price, hiding how it was produced.
- Refuted by: users who see the price name its source as aggregated opinion, with no misattribution, where misattribution would be measurable.

**A2 (alienation).** Traders who follow a displayed price, not their own information, are estranged from their judgement.
- Refuted by: decisions matching own-information forecasts equally with and without price exposure.

**A3 (panopticon).** A visible price makes traders or respondents regulate statements or trades, unaware who reads them.
- Refuted by: no difference between visible-price and hidden-price conditions, with information content held fixed.

**A4 (signs).** The price is a conventional sign whose meaning (probability) can come apart from its outcome.
- Refuted by: price-to-outcome calibration that stays stable across contract types and years.

## Not claimed

- That any market changes opinion, or that any concept above explains a market.
- That the Kalshi, Axis or Brennan figures are correct.
