# Heavy Qwen instrument: results

Generated by `workshop/ath_prompts/measure/heavy/report.py` from `data/<size>.json` (written by `run.py`). **There is no GPU on this machine** (4 CPU cores, 15 GB RAM). Every model here ran on CPU, under `nice -n 19` (2 threads; 3 for the 3B run), except where the size table says git-compute; the code picks a GPU unchanged when torch sees one. Weights live in a scratchpad and are not in the repository.

Scoring: teacher-forced, nothing generated. Each text is scored after one document-start token (`<|endoftext|>`), so every token, the first included, gets a surprisal; total bits is the whole text's information content under the model. Bits per character and per whitespace word do not depend on the tokenizer; bits per token mostly measures the tokenizer. Entropy is the model's predictive entropy before each token (what it expected to be surprised by). CIs are 95% paired bootstrap over prompts (10,000 resamples); p is the exact two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on per-prompt differences.

### Paired tests and verdict per claim

Verdict rule (`stats.verdict`): *holds at every size* = the CI excludes 0 in the claimed direction at every size run; *reverses* = CIs exclude 0 with opposite signs at two sizes; *shrinks with size* = CI excludes 0 at the smallest size, includes 0 at the largest, with a smaller point estimate; *fails at every size* = the CI excludes 0 against the claim at every size; *not resolved* = otherwise (CI includes 0). ## Verdicts

**No GPU.** This machine has none; 0.5B, 1.5B and 3B ran here on CPU, and the 7B (4-bit GGUF, llama-cpp-python) ran on git-compute (`workshop/compute/specs/heavy_qwen_7b.json`: 8 GitHub runners, 4 vCPU and 16 GB each, no GPU; weights downloaded inside the runners, results read back from the job logs with `compute_entry.py --assemble`). The same code runs on a GPU unchanged.

**B1, per sub-claim, across 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B and 7B-q4:**

- *Fewer tokens* (B1a): 236 vs 267, the same at every size because all four share one tokenizer. It is a tokenizer fact, not a model fact, and weak per prompt: informal is shorter in 7 of 10, longer in 2, tied in 1 (bootstrap CI of the mean difference excludes 0, Wilcoxon p = 0.11).
- *More information per unit* (B1b to B1d): **holds at every size**, in every unit. Per character 2.11 / 2.0 / 2.1 / 2.1 bits for informal against 1.46 / 1.42 / 1.44 / 1.59 for formal; informal is higher in 9 or 10 of 10 prompts at every size, Wilcoxon p ≤ 0.01. So the density result is not an artefact of the 0.5B model, nor of the tokenizer (per character and per word agree with per token).
- *Less total information for the same instruction* (B1e): **not supported**. Informal text costs MORE total bits than its formal rewrite at 0.5B, 1.5B and 3B (+25 to +35 bits per prompt, CI excludes 0), and the gap shrinks to +16 bits with a CI including 0 at 7B-q4. "Short but dense" is therefore the right reading, not "short and cheaper": the informal text is fewer tokens but carries at least as much surprise in total.
- *The model's own expectation*: predictive entropy is higher on informal text at every size (about +1 bit per token): the model knows it is on less familiar ground. The excess surprise beyond that expectation (surprisal − entropy) is positive at 0.5B to 3B and shrinks at 7B-q4 (CI includes 0). Part of the measured density is the model's unfamiliarity with the register (typos, "u", "bruh", dropped capitals), which a larger model partly absorbs; the per-character density itself does not shrink.
- *The preferences block* (one text): the scene-speak block costs more tokens (226 vs 204) and more bits at every size.

**Agreement across sizes.** The four sizes rank the 20 texts almost identically by bits per character (Spearman 0.92 to 0.99) and agree token by token (0.92 to 0.96). What is surprising to 0.5B is surprising to 7B. Total surprisal does not fall steadily with size on these short raw texts (no chat template); instruct models are scored here as plain language models, which favours no size.

**Logit lens (0.5B, 1.5B, 3B; the GGUF backend exposes no hidden states, so not 7B).** Prediction of the next token forms late. Through the first half of the network the lens surprisal of the actual token stays at 13 to 23 bits (about chance for a 151,665-token vocabulary, 17.2 bits, or worse), and most of the drop happens in the last quarter of the layers: the median token reaches half of its total information gain at depth 0.75 to 0.89. Informal tokens become predictable later than formal ones at every size (median half-information depth 0.83 / 0.82 / 0.89 against 0.79 / 0.75 / 0.86), and fewer of them are top-1 at the end (15 to 18% against 22 to 25%). Formal text is assembled earlier in the stack; informal text is resolved, when it is, only in the last layers.

**Human language against code ("interchangeable").** For the five statements, the asteroid claim costs about the same total bits as the English sentence (geometric-mean ratio 0.93 to 1.10 across sizes), though at a higher rate per character (3.3 to 3.6 bits/char against 2.0 to 2.4): the .ath form is shorter and packs the same information. The Lean statement costs about twice the bits of either (ratio 1.9 to 2.2), at the lowest rate per character (1.6 to 1.9): it is long, regular and predictable, but it spells out what English and .ath leave to definitions (the side conditions of the one-NOT theorem, fan-in, the evaluation semantics). Verdict: **English and asteroid are interchangeable in information content at every size; Lean is not, it carries about twice as much, because it states more.** Confound: the five triples were taken from the repository as written, not rewritten to equal content; the .ath forms lean on definitions (`base`, `slack`) and the English hello comments drop their subject.

## What this does not show

Ten prompts and one speaker; formal rewrites by one seat. Surprisal is not task success. Higher surprisal on informal text mixes information density with the model's bias against nonstandard spelling and register (the AAVE/scene caveat on the task board applies), and nothing here separates the two beyond the entropy/excess split. The 7B row is 4-bit quantised. Teacher-forced scoring of raw text, no chat template.
