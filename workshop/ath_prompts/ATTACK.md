# Attack on the prompting-method claims (C1 to C8 and the top-level conjecture)

Written 2026-11-25 by an attacking seat. The job was to argue against each claim, then check the argument on the data. Sources read for this file: `ath_prompt_method.md`, `workshop/ath_prompts/SYNTHESIS.md`, `workshop/ath_prompts/measure/trial/CLAUDE_HAIKU.md`, `trial/score_claude.py`, `trial/harness.py`, `trial/RESULTS.md`, `measure/cost_per_result.md`, `measure/heavy/results.md` and the stored data files. Scripts (standard library, no model calls) are in `workshop/ath_prompts/attack/`. Labels: checked-finite (exact, exhaustive), tested (sampled or one model run), analogy (a reading), open.

## What changed

- C3 (Haiku): "toss-up, no register effect found" became "toss-up, leaning against". Strict hand scoring: informal-only 0, formal-only 4 (p = 0.125); before, the published strict rule gave 1 vs 5 (p = 0.22) and was dismissed as a scorer artefact.
- C2: "more information per token" became "more surprisal per character". The gap survives a tokenizer-free model (+0.68 bits/char) and loses 20 to 25% to spelling and slang.
- C7: from untested to untested, leaning against: 73 to 86% of plain-seat tokens is a fixed floor.
- C8: from toss-up to untested: the data has no power to see a doubling.
- C4: the proxy is removed as evidence.
- C1: strengthened: shorter in characters (9 of 10) as well as tokens.

## Limits of this attack

- The hand codes for C3 were made by this seat with the register visible. They are listed with reasons in `attack/haiku_strict.py`.
- The Haiku floor is a different model from the Sonnet seats. Treating it as the Sonnet floor is an analogy.
- The character model is trained on Python documentation, which is formal technical English. Its bias points the same way as Qwen's.
- The noise-word list for C2 was chosen by reading the 10 prompts.
