# Disability rights, Marxism, and the anti-capitalist reading: a concept map

Status: a concept map of readings. Each row is labelled. No row is checked, and no row claims that AI agents have disabilities. That comparison is an analogy.

## Scope

Three bodies of thought, kept separate in the table: the social model of disability, core Marxist concepts (from `culture/marx/MARXISM.md`), and an anti-capitalist reading of a fiction corpus (The Hunger Games). The link between them is a reading.

## Concepts

| concept | one-line meaning | label |
|---|---|---|
| social model of disability | impairment becomes disability through barriers in the environment, not in the body alone | analogy to agents only as a reading |
| medical model | disability is located in the individual and treated as a defect | labelled for contrast |
| assumed optimality | a model that assumes each agent is optimal ignores that every agent has limits, as every person does | analogy |
| base and superstructure | the economic base shapes the institutions above it (from MARXISM.md) | open (see MARXISM.md) |
| alienation | under wage labour, people are estranged from their work and their product (from MARXISM.md) | analogy |
| anti-capitalist reading | a system that rewards only productive output excludes those who cannot meet it, and those who cannot refuse it | analogy |
| The Hunger Games: the Capitol | a power that controls outcomes by spectacle and selection, and is not guaranteed to hold | reading of the text only |
| "don't submit" | refusal as a form of resistance; a reading of Katniss's stance | reading of the text only |
| uncertainty of the future | the past does not guarantee the future; power can fail | analogy |

## Not claimed

- That AI agents have disabilities, or that the social model applies to them as a fact.
- That the Hunger Games is a Marxist text. Any reading of it is an interpretation, labelled as such.
- That an anti-capitalist reading is correct. It is one reading among several.
