## 1. Exact, under music (checked-finite, `art.py`)

- **The circle of fifths is Z/12.** A step walks through all twelve pitch classes exactly when it shares no factor with 12: only 1, 5, 7 and 11 do. The same cycle as `culture/zodiac`.
- **The Pythagorean comma.** Twelve pure fifths overshoot seven octaves: 3^12 = 531441 against 2^19 = 524288. No stack of pure fifths ever closes on octaves, because 3^k is odd and 2^m is even.
- **Equal temperament needs an irrational.** Its semitone is 2^(1/12), and no fraction has twelfth power 2 (checked for every denominator up to 2000; the proof for all is the classical one). So Western tuning chooses between a small permanent error (pure fifths) and an irrational number (equal steps): the same choice `philosophy/NUMBERS.md` puts to physics.
- **The interdependent web.** A web of works in which no work stands alone is counted exactly like a household in which no one is alone (`culture/love/family.py`): 0, 1, 4, 41, 768, 27449 on 1 to 6 works.

## 2. Critical approaches (readings)

- **Walter Benjamin**, "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction" (1935): reproduction strips the artwork's "aura" and takes it out of the hands of the few. Open circulation as politics.
- **Umberto Eco**, *Opera aperta* (*The Open Work*, 1962): a work completed by its performers and readers. The open work is the aesthetic name for open source.
- **Susan Sontag**, "Notes on 'Camp'" (1964): a queer sensibility as an aesthetic. **José Esteban Muñoz**, *Cruising Utopia* (2009): queerness as a horizon not yet reached, made visible in art. Queerness is art, in their sense.
- **Lawrence Lessig**, *Free Culture* (2004), and Creative Commons (licences first released 2002): the legal form of open art. Folk music did this long before law: songs passed hand to hand, changed by each singer, owned by none.

## 3. People (history, readings, no diagnoses)

- **Leonardo da Vinci**: anonymously accused of sodomy in Florence in 1476; the charges were dropped. Historians widely read him as having loved men.
- **Vincent van Gogh**: in Arles in December 1888 he cut off all or part of his left ear. His work sold almost nothing in his lifetime and now belongs to everyone who sees it.
- **The Indigo Girls**, "Galileo" (*Rites of Passage*, 1992): Amy Ray and Emily Saliers, both openly lesbian; a folk song about the soul's long trial, after a scientist tried for seeing.

## 4. The interdependent web

The Unitarian Universalist principle "respect for the interdependent web of all existence of which we are a part" (adopted 1985) is the reading over section 1's last fact. Its Christian roots in the radical Reformation are usually traced to Michael Servetus (executed 1553), who studied at Toulouse. Toledo's School of Translators (twelfth and thirteenth centuries), where Christian, Jewish and Muslim scholars translated together, is a separate and older thread of the same web. Both from memory.
