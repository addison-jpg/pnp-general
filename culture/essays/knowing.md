# Knowing Yourself and Knowing Others

*By Addison. Draft 2. No lyrics are quoted except one credited line, marked unverified. Next: formalize the argument in ath.*

---

There is a trade I kept making without noticing it. The more time I spent knowing myself, the less time I spent knowing anyone else. It felt like progress, because the mirror always answers. It answers in your own voice, which is the problem.

Paul says it plainly: "For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face" (1 Corinthians 13:12, KJV). The glass is a mirror. Seeing yourself in it is dim and partial. Seeing someone face to face is the other kind of knowing, the kind that can surprise you.

## The mirror

Monomania is an old word for an obsession with one thing. A mirror taken far enough becomes monomania: the whole world turns into a reflection of one fixation, and other people become props in it. Art can push you there. You understand a songwriter completely, or think you do, and he will never understand you back. The understanding runs one way. It is a mirror that happens to have someone else's face painted on it.

## The party where you go to die

The opposite of a mirror is a crowd. A party is full of faces that do not reflect you. Everyone throws a funeral, everyone throws a party; at the right party they are the same event. You arrive as one person and leave as another, and something ends in between. Bringing monomania to a party is bringing the mirror into the one room full of faces. Either the fixation breaks on contact, or it keeps you alone in the middle of everyone. I have done both. The first is better.

## How to leave town

Car Seat Headrest's *How to Leave Town* is a manual for exiting, and the exit is never clean. One title on it says the whole problem: *I Want You to Know That I'm Awake / I Hope That You're Asleep*. Wanting to be known, and hoping not to be seen, at the same time. That is what the mirror protects. It lets you be known only by yourself, where nobody can look back.

## Heaven full of people

Leaving town has a final form, which is leaving the world. The songs (on *2* and again on *Disjecta Membra*) close that door: if heaven is full of people, it will be exactly like earth. Wherever you go, if others are there, the problem comes with you, because the problem was never the place.

Sartre said hell is other people (*Huis clos*, 1944). This is gentler and truer. Other people do not make hell. They make earth, ordinary life, the only life there is. Beauvoir is closer: you are free only in a situation, among others, never alone. A heaven without people would be the mirror forever, monomania with no end. A heaven with people is just this again. So face to face is not the consolation prize for failing to escape. It is the whole thing.

## The father, the lineage, the milk

Before anyone chooses who to face, they are handed a lineage. Genesis and Matthew list it as a chain of names, each one begat by the last, nobody asked. *Kid War* recites a lineage like that and then breaks it with plain anger at a father. *Please Pass the Milk Please* stays at the dinner table, in the small politeness that holds an ugly family together, and its title sounds like Raymond Carver's *Will You Please Be Quiet, Please?* (1976). The resemblance is mine to point out; I have not confirmed the influence. Carver's people sit inside ordinary rooms where nothing is said and everything is understood.

That is where the mirror comes from. You learn to look at yourself in a house where looking at each other was dangerous. The lineage is the first situation, in Beauvoir's sense: not chosen, not escapable by wishing, only by acting.

## Shedding

"My anger sharpens to a point and sheds my skin." (Car Seat Headrest, "High to Death", *Twin Fantasy*. Line as I remember it; check before this goes anywhere.)

Anger does not have to blur into resentment. It can narrow into something sharp enough to cut the old skin off. *Twin Fantasy* exists twice: *Mirror to Mirror* in 2011 and *Face to Face* in 2018, the same songs recorded again by the same person seven years older. He went back to his own mirror and remade it facing out. That is not only a metaphor. People do it.

## Scattered fragments

*Disjecta membra* means scattered limbs, the fragments left of something whole. A self is made like that: pieces of a father you did not choose, songs you understood one way, a party, a verse about a glass. You do not get one clean origin. You get fragments, and you put them together facing other people.

Those days are long resolved. I am happier than ever. The mirror broke and I would not miss it. The skin is shed, and what is left is the face, turned outward, in a world that is going to be full of people either way.
