# w4: mixed downstream collision (h = x_a v x_b)  -- PARTIAL: no proof, no counterexample
Setup: S in Pa (C1: N:=1, point z_{S+a+b}, atoms e_u), S' in Pb (C0: N:=0, point z_S', atoms e_u+e_a), S n S' = {c}, g=p|q downstream of N.

## Proved (paper)
1. Real-circuit facts at a mixed collision. h=1 at e_a, e_b, e_a+e_u, so C0 = real there; h(e_c)=0 so C1 = real at e_c.
 - g in A0(S'): g0(z_S')=g0(e_a)=0 (a not in S'), so REAL g(e_a)=0 although g1(e_a)=1 (a in B1(g), from the S-merge, which joins {a},{c}: WLOG c in B1(p), a in B1(q), B1(g) subset {a,b,c}). So g genuinely depends on N: g1(e_a)=1 != g0(e_a)=0.
 - B0(g) subset S' (since e_u+e_a <= z_S' iff u notin S'); the S'-merge gives two distinct u',v' in S' with real g(e_u'+e_a)=g(e_v'+e_a)=1, while real g(e_a)=0. Also real g(e_c)=g1(e_c)=1.
 - Hence q (the input carrying a in C1) has q1(e_a)=1, q0(e_a)=0, and q (or p) must be N-dependent in a way that N=1 -> 1 at e_a but N=0 -> 0: N is a "switch" at e_a. Since g1(0)=0, N cannot be an OR-reader (OR(N,y) is 1 in C1, never in A1): the N-to-g path begins with an AND reader N&y, with y(e_a)=1 in C1 terms.
 - So the first reader r = N&y on the path is a gate that is NEVER charged (AND) and sits at depth 1; but it is only one gate, shared by all collisions through it, so it pays one collision at most; this is the precise form of the Hall obstruction.
2. Freedom not yet exploited that is real: at an AND gate, the union-find for S may follow ANY input lying in A(S) (the invariant needs only B(AND) subset B(input in A(S))). So each S can use a sub-cone C(S) (choose one A(S)-input per AND). A collision at g needs g in C(S) n C(S'). A collision can be forced only if every AND on every route from g to o_S has its other input outside A(S) (value 1 at the test point, e.g. N | stuff in C1). That is exactly the situation of the "y = N | (a|c)" gates: in C1 these are identically 1, hence never in A1(S), forcing the route through g.
3. Hand analysis of a forced-route circuit (n=3, S={a,c}, S'={b,c}): o_S = g & (N|a|c), o_S' = g & (N|b|c) forces g = a|b|c on all points (by correctness), i.e. g is a function of leaves; writing g = B | (N&c) (B=a|b, N=~B) makes it downstream of N, but then g0(e_a)=g(e_a)=1, so g is NOT in A0(S'): no mixed collision. A mixed collision needs real g(e_a)=0 while o_S(e_a)=1, forcing o_S to obtain a at e_a from a route other than g; with that route in A1(S) the AND-choice (item 2) leaves g, or the route is an OR whose merge happens in a different gate. Heuristic: this suggests (not proved) the collision is avoidable by choosing sub-cones, but making this rigorous requires showing the alternate route lies in A1(S) (it is 1 at e_a in C1 only because of real correctness at e_a, which is a C0 statement) -- the same C0/C1 mismatch as before.

## Remaining gap (precise)
Show: if g in C1-cone(S) n C0-cone(S') with real g(e_a)=0, g1(e_a)=1 and N-first-reader AND r = N&y, then either (i) some AND on the route g -> o_S (or g -> o_S') has its other input in A(S)/A'(S') (re-route, collision disappears), or (ii) an extra uncharged gate exists. The missing ingredient is information on y and on the other AND inputs in the C1 world at points where h=1 (e_a, e_a+e_c): correctness gives only the C0 values there. No lemma of the form "mixed collisions <= #uncharged ORs + #ANDs, injectively" is established.
