# Non-discrete physics: literature map for the owner's idea

checker-checker, 2026-11-24. Layer C (premises and maps): nothing here enters a Lean statement.
Five Sonnet research agents, about 50 web searches, abstracts and summaries only (no full texts

**The idea (owner, verbatim in substance).** Every physical sub-component (quarks, neutrons,
positrons, atoms) is variable (size, intensity, ...); so everything in physics is fields and waves,
and discrete conceptualizations do not properly exist; this extends to quantum gravity.

Labels: **[THEOREM]** proved under stated hypotheses; **[CONJECTURE]**; **[POSITION]**
(interpretive or philosophical); **[NON-MAINSTREAM]** (outside peer review or single-author
programs outside the mainstream).

## 1. Verdict

1. **Strongly supported: there are no fundamental, localizable, observer-independent particles.**
   Several theorems (section 2) and the mainstream reading of QFT (particles are emergent,
   regime-dependent excitations of fields).
2. **Not supported: "properties vary continuously, no discreteness exists".** In every case found,
   discrete quantum numbers survive (charge, spin class, baryon number as a topological integer;
   field excitations are quantized in integer steps). Quantum fields are operator-valued, not
   classical waves, so "everything is waves" is not literally the content of QFT.
3. **Quantum gravity: open, no mainstream program asserts the idea.** Continuum is the default in
   asymptotic safety, string theory and gauge/gravity; discreteness is built in (causal sets) or
   derived and contested (loop quantum gravity). One result helps the extension: in
   background-independent quantum gravity a global particle notion may be unavailable
   (Colosi and Rovelli).
4. **Explicit gravity-inclusive "particles are field structures" conjectures exist, outside the
   mainstream:** Burinskii's Kerr-Newman soliton electron; Wheeler's geons; Einstein-Rosen bridges;
   Bartnik-McKinnon particle-like Einstein-Yang-Mills solutions (a theorem, but the solutions are
   unstable).
5. **Complexity side, under the board premise "a physical model is an oracle world":** a continuum
   model adds power only if exact real numbers are physical; then its efficient class is
   non-uniform (P/poly-type), so "NP is efficient in exact-real physics" is the negation of
   L-gen-101. Every realistic route (noise, finite precision, finite information) falls back to
   P or BQP. See section 6.

## 2. QFT: no particles at the fundamental level

- [THEOREM] Malament, "In Defense of Dogma: Why There Cannot Be a Relativistic Quantum Mechanics of
  (Localizable) Particles", in Clifton (ed.), Perspectives on Quantum Reality (1996),
  doi:10.1007/978-94-015-8656-6_1. No relativistic quantum theory of localizable particles, under
  translation covariance, energy bounded below and microcausality. SUPPORTS; targets sharp
  localization only.
- [THEOREM] Halvorson and Clifton, "No place for particles in relativistic quantum theories?",
  Philosophy of Science 69 (2002), arXiv:quant-ph/0103041. Closes loopholes in Malament; the
  authors still explain particle detections from fields. SUPPORTS; LIMITS "only fields".
- [THEOREM] Halvorson, "Reeh-Schlieder defeats Newton-Wigner", Philosophy of Science 68 (2001),
  arXiv:quant-ph/0007060. Alternative localization schemes fail too. SUPPORTS.
- [POSITION, on Haag's theorem] D. Fraser, "The fate of 'particles' in quantum field theories with
  interactions", Stud. Hist. Phil. Mod. Phys. 39 (2008) 841-859, doi:10.1016/j.shpsb.2008.05.003.
  Interacting QFT has no Fock/particle interpretation. SUPPORTS; cutoff QFT evades Haag.
- [THEOREM / physical result] Fulling (1973), Davies (1975), Unruh (1976): particle number depends
  on the observer's acceleration. Reviews: arXiv:gr-qc/0205078, arXiv:0710.5373. SUPPORTS "particles
  are observer-relative"; the field and its vacuum are what is invariant.
- [POSITION] Hobson, "There are no particles, there are only fields", Am. J. Phys. 81 (2013) 211,
  doi:10.1119/1.4789885, arXiv:1204.4616. The most direct statement of the idea. Rebuttals in the
  same journal: Sassoli de Bianchi, "Quantum fields are not fields", arXiv:1204.6384.
- [POSITION] Sebens, "The fundamentality of fields", Synthese (2022), arXiv:2202.09425. SUPPORTS.
- [POSITION] Wallace, "In defence of naivete", Synthese 151 (2006), arXiv:quant-ph/0112148:
  particles are emergent, approximate, still real. LIMITS "do not exist".
- [POSITION] Baker, "Against field interpretations of quantum field theory", Brit. J. Phil. Sci.
  60 (2009) 585-609 (DOI not seen): the wavefunctional and Fock pictures are unitarily equivalent,
  so the anti-particle arguments also hit the field picture. LIMITS strongly.
- [POSITION] Davies, "Particles do not exist" (1984), **unverified** (title seen only second-hand).

## 3. Variable particle properties

- [CONJECTURE] Georgi, "Unparticle physics", Phys. Rev. Lett. 98 (2007) 221601,
  arXiv:hep-ph/0703260. A scale-invariant sector behaving like a non-integer number of particles;
  mathematically a generalized free field with continuous mass spectrum (arXiv:0806.2009). Not
  observed. The closest established object to "variable mass and number".
- [THEOREM-level] Infraparticles: Buchholz and Roberts, Commun. Math. Phys. 330 (2014),
  arXiv:1304.2794; Kulish and Faddeev (1970, dressed QED states; seen second-hand). Charged particles
  in QED have no sharp mass shell. SUPPORTS; the charge stays sharp.
- [THEOREM-level] Resonances as Gamow vectors with complex energy (arXiv:quant-ph/0201091):
  unstable particles have no sharp mass; pole mass and width are still definite numbers.
- [THEOREM] Schuster and Toro, continuous-spin particles, JHEP 09 (2013) 104, arXiv:1302.1198: the
  most general massless Poincaré representations have a continuous parameter. Not observed.
- [POSITION, standard physics] Skyrmions: Adkins, Nappi and Witten, Nucl. Phys. B228 (1983) 552,
  doi:10.1016/0550-3213(83)90559-x. Nucleons as solitons of the pion field: a particle as an
  extended field configuration; baryon number is a topological integer (discrete).
- Not verified this round: running masses and couplings, proton radius / form factors.

## 4. Quantum gravity: continuum or discrete

- [CONJECTURE] Asymptotic safety (Reuter, arXiv:hep-th/9605030): gravity as a continuum quantum
  field theory at a non-Gaussian fixed point; evidence only in truncations. SUPPORTS most directly.
- [POSITION] Dittrich and Thiemann, J. Math. Phys. 50 (2009) 012503, arXiv:0708.1721: kinematical
  discreteness of LQG spectra may not survive at the physical level. Rovelli's reply:
  arXiv:0708.2481. Open. SUPPORTS partly.
- [POSITION] Rovelli and Smolin, discrete area and volume, arXiv:gr-qc/9411005. LIMITS.
- [THEOREM] Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin, "Discreteness without symmetry breaking: a theorem", Mod.
  Phys. Lett. A 24 (2009) 2579, arXiv:gr-qc/0605006: causal-set discreteness is compatible with
  Lorentz invariance. LIMITS (relativity does not force a continuum).
- [POSITION] Collins et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 93 (2004) 191301, arXiv:gr-qc/0403053: lattice-like
  discreteness with a preferred frame needs fine-tuning. SUPPORTS against naive lattices only.
- [POSITION] Hossenfelder, Living Rev. Relativity 16 (2013) 2, arXiv:1203.6191: survey; treats
  "no minimal length" as live.
- [THEOREM-adjacent] Colosi and Rovelli, "What is a particle?", Class. Quantum Grav. 26 (2009)
  025002, arXiv:gr-qc/0409054: global particles vs local detector particles; in background-
  independent quantum gravity a global particle notion may be unavailable. SUPPORTS the extension.
- Partanen and Tulkki, Rep. Prog. Phys. 88 (2025) 057802, arXiv:2310.01460: gravity as a
  continuous gauge field on flat spacetime; renormalizability shown at first order only.
- [POSITION] Huggett and Wüthrich, arXiv:1206.6290; Crowther, arXiv:1410.0345: spacetime may not be
  fundamental at all, continuous or discrete. Not found: a philosopher defending a fundamental
  continuum on the owner's grounds.

## 5. Particles as structures of fields or geometry (historical and outside the mainstream)

- [THEOREM, solutions unstable] Bartnik and McKinnon (1988): particle-like Einstein-Yang-Mills
  solitons exist only with gravity coupled; review arXiv:hep-th/9810070.
- [POSITION] Wheeler, "Geons", Phys. Rev. 97 (1955) 511, doi:10.1103/PhysRev.97.511: mass without
  mass.
- [POSITION] Einstein and Rosen, "The particle problem in the general theory of relativity", Phys.
  Rev. 48 (1935) 73, doi:10.1103/physrev.48.73.
- [THEOREM, rigour contested] Einstein, Infeld and Hoffmann, Ann. Math. 39 (1938) 65,
  doi:10.2307/1968714: motion of singularities from the field equations.
- [NON-MAINSTREAM] Burinskii, "Kerr-Newman electron as spinning soliton", arXiv:1410.2888;
  arXiv:1505.03440: the electron as an extended gravitating soliton. **The explicit
  gravity-inclusive conjecture the owner expected.**
- [NON-MAINSTREAM] Mead, "Collective electrodynamics I", PNAS 94 (1997) 6013,
  doi:10.1073/pnas.94.12.6013: electromagnetism from coupled quantum waves, no point particles.
- [NON-MAINSTREAM, fringe] Wolff, "Wave Structure of Matter", Physics Essays 6 (1993) 181
  (**unverified**). Do not cite as established.

## 6. What it gives on the complexity side (oracle-world premise)

- [THEOREM] Pour-El and Richards (1981): the 3D wave equation has computable initial data with a
  non-computable solution (continuous, not C^1, data; sup norm). Computability, not polynomial time.
- [THEOREM] Blum, Shub and Smale (Bull. AMS 1989): P vs NP over the reals is a different question;
  no known implication for the Turing question.
- [THEOREM] Siegelmann and Sontag (Theoret. Comput. Sci. 131, 1994; **from memory, verify**):
  analog networks with real weights compute exactly P/poly in polynomial time. The real weight is
  non-uniform advice. Under the premise, the "exact-real continuum world" is a P/poly world, and
  "NP is efficient there" is NP ⊆ P/poly, the negation of L-gen-101.
- [POSITION] Vergis, Steiglitz and Dickinson (Math. Comput. Simul. 28, 1986,
  doi:10.1016/0378-4754(86)90105-9); Aaronson, arXiv:quant-ph/0502072: with noise and finite
  precision, analog devices are polynomially simulable; no physical NP solver.
- [THEOREM] Jordan, Lee and Preskill, Science 336 (2012) 1130, arXiv:1111.3633: phi^4 scattering in
  BQP. A field theory simulable on a lattice adds nothing beyond BQP.
- [POSITION] Lloyd, Nature 406 (2000), arXiv:quant-ph/9908043 (Bekenstein bound); Gisin, Erkenntnis
  (2019), arXiv:1803.06824: finite information per region; the infinite-information real number
  that gives the continuum its power may not exist.

## 8. Follow-up: access rules for real-number oracles (verified literature + Lean)

**Literature (one Sonnet agent; abstracts and secondary sources, full texts blocked).** The map is
known in substance: Siegelmann and Sontag (Theoret. Comput. Sci. 131, 1994): real-weight analog
nets in polynomial time = P/poly; Balcázar, Gavaldà and Siegelmann (IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 43,

as oracles", Proc. R. Soc. A 464, 2008; part II doi:10.1098/rspa.2008.0412): infinite, unbounded
and fixed precision protocols; P/poly attained only with non-computable protocols, BPP//log* an
upper bound for a broad class of experiments (**to verify**). No source found with the explicit
sentence "NP efficient in continuum physics iff NP ⊆ P/poly, i.e. not L-gen-101".
Lead to verify: if realistic experiments give only BPP//log*, then "NP efficient by realistic
continuum measurement" puts NP in a logarithmic-advice class, much closer to P = NP than P/poly.
