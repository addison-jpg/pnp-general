# Specieshood, specialized: one recent primary paper per animal

Addison asked for the specialized, holistic view of specieshood: fly brains, dolphins, apes, mice. This file lists one recent primary paper for each. Nothing here models a brain.

**Read status.** Every line below comes from the OpenAlex record (title, year, DOI, venue, first author) and the OpenAlex abstract only, fetched 2026-11-25 from api.openalex.org. The full papers were not read. Each headline number is quoted from the abstract. Label: cited (abstract read, paper not read).

## Notes

- Scope differs by animal. The fly and mouse papers are wiring maps (one whole brain; part of one cortex). The dolphin paper is gene expression in stranded animals. The ape paper is genomes, not brains. A holistic comparison across these four would compare different kinds of data. That gap is open.
- The fly and mouse numbers are not comparable as brain sizes: the mouse figure covers a cortical volume, not the whole brain.
- Dolphin choice: a behavioural paper was also found (Sayigh et al., "Bottlenose dolphin mothers modify signature whistles in the presence of their own calves", PNAS 2023, 10.1073/pnas.2300262120). Its abstract states the result (higher maximum frequencies and wider frequency ranges with own calves) but gives no number, so it was not used as the one paper.
- How they were found: OpenAlex title search, filtered to 2023 to 2026 and to articles with abstracts. "Recent" and "one per animal" are this seat's choice. Others would be as good.
