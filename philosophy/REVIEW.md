Verdict key: fine = prose matches the theorem. overclaim = true theorem, prose says it captures more than it does.
vacuous = the statement is empty or a restatement of its hypothesis. misattribution = the philosopher is
credited with something the text or the model does not support.

General pattern (applies to most rows): every theorem is a short lemma about orders, lists, sets or linear
maps. Each file opens with an honest "claims nothing about what X meant" disclaimer. The doc comments then
name theorems after the philosopher's idea ("capitalist_realism", "each_seeks_the_death_of_the_other",
"unity_and_identity_of_opposites"). The name does the interpretive work and the Lean does not. The disclaimer
does not cover that, because the claim is made by the theorem name and the bold doc line, not by the header.

## Notes on the mathematics

- Every theorem in Deleuze.lean and Fisher.lean means what its Lean statement says. I found no wrong statement. Hypotheses are not empty: Fisher's concrete frame `N` and Bridge's `Fin 2` tables instantiate them. `nondegenerate_isolated` is the only theorem with real analytic content and it is a standard theorem.
- Weakest definitions: `Problem` (an arbitrary function, so "problem" carries no meaning), `Haunted` (any branching), `Recognise` (a common upper bound), `Free` (two incomparable futures), `dimension` (a rename of `finrank`), `Barbarian` (a rename of `Other`). Theorems about them are short unfoldings.
- `Bridge.actual` and `Bridge.Problem` are restated from Deleuze.lean but `actual` is never used, so the claim that Bridge parallels `distinct_problems_same_actual` is by analogy only.
- The one definitional-identity in the Bridge file (`Phi_eq_PhiT := rfl`) and the "arbitrary tables" caveat in its header are honest. The over-reach is in the README (row 25) and in how UNIFY.md describes the Bridge results (row 35).

## Overall verdict

The Lean is correct and compiles, and every file carries an honest top-of-file disclaimer, but the interpretive weight sits in theorem names and bold doc lines (`capitalist_realism`, `each_seeks_the_death_of_the_other`, `unity_and_identity_of_opposites`, `unified`), and those claims are not supported: nearly all of them are one-line order, list or set facts that would be provable without any philosophy. Three statements are outright wrong or empty: root README.md says "Φ is not determined by the table" (it is, by `rfl`), `barbarian_whatever_is_right` and `realize_mem` say nothing, and the "Beauvoir's ambiguity", "Hegel's determinate negation" and "Deleuze's actual/virtual in the tree" labels are the author's, not the texts'. Keep the theorems, rename them for their mathematical content, and move the philosophical readings into clearly marked "our reading" remarks.
