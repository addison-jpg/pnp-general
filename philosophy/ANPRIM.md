# An anarcho-primitivist lens, read critically

Labels: **checked-finite** (exact over a stated finite set), **analogy** (a reading), **open**. Every source is cited from memory; none was re-read for this file. Frames: `philosophy/anprim.ath` (`bin/asteroid --no-lean philosophy/anprim.ath`).

This file reads a tradition and then criticises it. It condemns political violence without qualification. It contains nothing operational.

## 1. The sources

**Jacques Ellul**, *La Technique ou l'enjeu du siecle* (1954; *The Technological Society*, 1964). Technique, the totality of methods chosen for efficiency, becomes autonomous. It answers to no value but its own growth, and absorbs politics, economy and the person. Ellul was a Christian anarchist and a non-violent critic.

**Marshall Sahlins**, "Notes on the Original Affluent Society" (Man the Hunter symposium, 1966) and *Stone Age Economics* (1972). Foragers met modest wants with a few hours of work a day: affluence by wanting little.
The later debate: counts of working hours left out food processing, tool making and childcare; seasonal hunger and high child mortality were understated (Hawkes, Kaplan and others; cited from memory). The thesis survives as a challenge to "progress" stories, not as a measured fact. **Open.**

**John Zerzan**, *Future Primitive* (1994). Domestication, agriculture, number, even symbolic thought are the start of alienation; the aim is a return to forager life.

## 2. The critique of primitivism

`culture/race/RACE.md` section 7: romanticising "primitive" peoples is a racist move. It turns living Indigenous people into a picture of humanity's past. Any anarcho-primitivism that does not face this repeats the gaze it claims to escape. Foragers are contemporaries with their own politics and futures, not evidence for someone else's longing.

## 3. "Industrial Society and Its Future" (Kaczynski, published 1995): a critical reading

Described in my own words, not quoted.

**What it claims.**
- *The technological system.* Industrial technology forms one system that grows by its own logic, cannot be reformed piece by piece, and narrows human freedom.
- *The power process.* People need goals that take real effort, achieved by their own effort, with autonomy. Modern life denies this for most needs.
- *Surrogate activities.* So people pour themselves into goals they set up artificially (careers, hobbies, science, activism). The text treats these as signs of a frustrated power process.
- *Conclusion.* The system must be brought down, not reformed. The text also spends long passages attacking "leftists" in psychological terms.

**Where it overlaps mainstream critique.** The autonomy of technique is Ellul's thesis, stated decades earlier. Worries about alienated work, surveillance and ecological harm are shared by many writers who never endorsed violence. Overlap with a respectable idea does not make the conclusion respectable.

**Where it fails, logically.**
- *One system.* It treats all technology as a single indivisible thing, so that reform of any part is ruled out by definition. That is an assumption presented as a finding.
- *Surrogate activity is unfalsifiable.* Any meaningful pursuit that does not fit the theory gets relabelled a surrogate. A category that absorbs every counterexample explains nothing.
- *Ad hominem.* Explaining opponents by their supposed psychology does not answer their arguments.
- *A false choice.* It offers only total system or total collapse, and skips everything between.

**Where it fails, empirically.**
- Industrial systems have been reformed in part: pollution law, ozone treaties, labour law. The "no reform" premise is contradicted by history.
- Billions of people live on industrial agriculture and medicine. A collapse would kill the poor, the sick, the disabled and the young first. A forager economy cannot support the present population.
- Its strategy failed on its own terms: the bombings persuaded no movement into being, and the manifesto's publication led to the author's identification and arrest in 1996.

**The violence.** Between 1978 and 1995 Kaczynski mailed or placed bombs that killed 3 people and injured 23 (cited from memory; I am confident of the 3 deaths and the 1978 to 1995 span; some accounts count 24 injured, so the injury count is not certain here). These were murders of people who had harmed no one. Nothing in the text excuses them. The violence is condemned plainly and without qualification. The ideas in the text, where they have merit, came from others first, who never needed violence.

## 4. The formal argument against his strategy

**Checked-finite** (`philosophy/cone.py`, every base graph on up to 6 vertices, 33,867 graphs; see `philosophy/PSYCHOSIS.md` section 5). Put one hub over a base: the cone.
- The hub is critical (removing it disconnects everything) exactly when the base is disconnected.
- A link from the hub to one person is critical exactly when that person has no lateral link.
- The fewest lateral links that make the hub not critical: the number of pieces of the base minus one.

**Analogy (a reading):** let the hub be the industrial system and the base be people. Where people depend on the system and not on each other, the base is in pieces. Attacking the hub then cuts everyone off, and the cut falls hardest on those with no lateral links at all: the isolated, the poor, the sick. The attack harms the people it claims to free, first and most.

The non-violent alternative follows from the same fact: build lateral links (mutual aid, cooperatives, community, care) until the hub is not critical. Then no one depends on one centre, and the centre can be changed without anyone being cut off. This is slower and it is the only route the graph allows that harms no one.

## 5. Forward, not back: Mark Fisher

Mark Fisher, "Acid Communism" (unfinished introduction, published in *k-punk*, 2018; cited from memory). The way out of capitalist realism is collective and forward-looking: consciousness raised together, the counterculture's sense that the world could be freer, carried forward. That is the opposite of primitivism's return.

- **Analogy:** history as a 2-chain (`core/ontology/asymmetry.py`, checked-finite: every non-symmetric preorder on 1 to 4 worlds maps onto it). There is no way back to an earlier world. The choice is what to build next, and with whom.
