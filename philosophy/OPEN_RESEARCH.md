# The open-research imperative: a postmodern argument

This is an argument, stated as strongly as its sources allow. It is a **position**, not a result: nothing in it is checked, except where it points at a check that already exists.

## The claim

Research that matters to many people should be open: its data, its code, its failures and its labels, readable and re-runnable by anyone. Postmodern philosophy does not weaken this demand. It is where the demand comes from.

## The argument

1. **No result is legitimate by authority alone.** Lyotard, *La Condition postmoderne* (1979; cited from memory): the grand narratives that once legitimated knowledge (the progress of reason, the emancipation of humanity) have lost their credibility; this is the "incredulity toward metanarratives". A result can no longer be true because an institution, a journal or a famous name says so.
2. **Then legitimacy has to come from somewhere local and checkable.** If not from a story about where knowledge comes from, then from the fact that anyone can test it. A claim with a recheck command that anyone can run is legitimate in a way that needs no metanarrative. This repository's rule is exactly that: every row of `RESULTS.md` names its evidence, and most name a command that reruns it.
3. **Lyotard drew this conclusion himself.** His book ends (cited from memory) by recommending that the public be given free access to the memory and data banks. Read today, that is open data and open code.
4. **Closed research is a star.** When every result passes through one hub (a journal, a lab, a company), removing or capturing the hub cuts everything off: in `philosophy/cone.py`'s exact terms, the hub is critical exactly when the parts have no lateral links. Open research adds the lateral links: anyone can reach anyone's data. Enough of them flatten the cone into a disk, where no single centre is critical (`philosophy/PSYCHOSIS.md` section 5; the graph fact is checked-finite, the reading is analogy).
5. **Science already holds this norm.** Merton's norms of science (1942; cited from memory) include communism (findings are common property) and organised scepticism (every claim is open to testing). Openness is not an add-on to rigour; it is how rigour is done in public.
6. **Failures belong in the open too.** A refuted claim kept with its counterexample stops the next person from repeating it. This repository keeps every refuted row (`RESULTS.md`, six of them) and every dead end (`one-not/DEAD_ENDS.md`). A postmodern science is not one without errors; it is one that shows them.
7. **Context belongs in the open.** Forsythe's legacy (Unertl, Abraham and Bakken, *JAMIA* 2021, doi:10.1093/jamia/ocaa337) and the guidance for reporting qualitative research (Ancker et al., *JAMIA* 2021, doi:10.1093/jamia/ocab195) both say that human experience and context are data, and must be reported for the work to be judged. Open research includes the readings, labelled as readings.

## Limits

- Some data must stay closed: anything that identifies a person, especially health data. Openness of method and of aggregate results is compatible with that; this repository holds no personal data.
- "Open" is not "true". An open claim can still be wrong. Openness only makes the error findable.
- This file is an argument. Its graph step (4) is checked; everything else is a reading of cited texts, most of them not re-read here.
